\begin{abstract-en}
[Context] A business rule lives in three places that can drift apart: the organisational requirement,
the executable Gherkin specification, and the code. Behaviour-Driven Development (BDD) assumes
executable specifications keep requirements and code aligned, yet no metric checks whether that
alignment still holds. [Objective] We ask whether the specification layer of a Cucumber-style suite
still acts as an \emph{oracle}---discriminating changes in the business rules it describes---and we
design an instrument for organisations to answer this about their own suites. [Method] Following
Design Science Research, we build and evaluate a drift-detection protocol via mutation-based oracle
measurement feasibility in two ecosystems and a public-population screen from a frame of \Nframe{}
repositories drawn with a fixed seed under a categorised attrition protocol.
[Results] Mutation-based oracle measurement is feasible in Java and JavaScript/TypeScript, with
per-mutant coverage and per-scenario attribution. However, of \Nscreened{} screened repositories,
\Neligible{} met static eligibility (\YieldPct\,\%; 95\,\% CI \YieldLo--\YieldHi\,\%), and of
\Nbuildtested{} build-tested repositories, \Ngreen{} produced a green, executing Gherkin baseline.
[Conclusion/Contributions] The binding constraint on BDD oracle evidence is infrastructural rather
than statistical: public BDD repositories are overwhelmingly frameworks, templates and demonstrators,
not applications with an executable specification layer. We contribute the instrument, feasibility
evidence and quantified attrition analysis. \emph{Limitation.} Without independent human raters,
judgement-coded indicators carry no inter-rater reliability statistic, and no corpus-scale
association was estimated.
\end{abstract-en}
